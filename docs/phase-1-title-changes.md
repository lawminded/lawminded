# Phase 1: what changed in the titles Google shows

**26 August 2026 — staged on the branch, not yet live.**

Your site is indexed and ranking. The problem is that people see it in
Google and do not click: 4,136 impressions produced 59 clicks over 28
days. Part of the reason was a bug. Titles were being shortened to fit
Google, and the shortening cut them off mid-sentence, so a page could
appear in search as "Auditor Appointment, Rotation & Removal: ADT-1,
Sections" — which reads as broken. Separately, every one of the 55
document-format pages had a title too long for Google to display, so the
end of each document name was being chopped off.

## The numbers

| | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pages with a title too long for Google | 61 | 0 |
| Article titles ending mid-phrase | 105 | 27 |
| Average title length | 62 | 53 |
| Titles changed | — | 154 of 204 |

The 27 that still end mid-phrase are headlines with no comma or colon in
the right place. Nothing can tell automatically that "Raise" is a bad place
to stop while "Removal" is fine, so the pages that matter were written by
hand instead. The 27 left are low-traffic pages.

## What to expect, honestly

Titles change what Google *displays*, which changes how many people click.
They do not directly change where you rank. Google usually re-crawls and
updates a displayed title within days to a few weeks.

One caveat worth stating up front. The query "difference between rti and
pil" already ranks **4.6** — top five — and gets **zero** clicks. That
pattern normally means Google's AI answer box is answering the question
above the results, and no title rewrite recovers those clicks. Some share
of the gap is structural.

The test to run is a fresh Search Console export 28 days after this goes
live. If article click-through moves from 0.73% toward 2.5%, the theory
held and Phase 2 is worth starting. If it does not move, the AI-answer
explanation is the dominant one and the plan needs rethinking rather than
continuing.
