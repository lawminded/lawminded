A contract can look complete — signed, stamped, full of clauses — and still collapse the moment it is tested, because of something nobody noticed at signing. The gaps that sink Indian agreements are predictable, and avoiding them costs nothing at the drafting stage.

The mistakes that hurt most are vague terms, missing essential clauses, inadequate stamping, void restraints of trade under Section 27, e-signing documents that cannot legally be e-signed, and silence on dispute resolution.

The bottom line

The quiet killer: inadequate stamping, which can make an otherwise valid contract inadmissible as evidence until the duty and penalty are paid.

The false comfort: a broad post-employment non-compete, generally void under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act.

The one people do not know: wills, powers of attorney, trust deeds, negotiable instruments other than cheques, and sale deeds of immovable property cannot be validly e-signed.

Vague and ambiguous terms

If a term can be read two ways, it will be — by whichever side it favours.

Vague scope such as "provide marketing support", undefined timelines such as "promptly", and subjective standards such as "to the client's satisfaction" are the most litigated parts of any contract.

The fix is concreteness: specific deliverables, dates, amounts, and acceptance criteria somebody could actually test. A contract's job is to remove ambiguity, not to preserve it for later.

Missing essential clauses

Many disputes trace back to a clause that simply was not there.

The usual omissions are payment triggers stating when money is actually due, IP ownership of work product, a termination clause, liability limits, confidentiality, and dispute resolution.

A contract silent on these leaves the parties arguing about the default position, which is nobody's preferred outcome. Draft from a clause checklist so nothing essential drops out.

No clear consideration

Under the Indian Contract Act an agreement generally needs consideration — something of value exchanged — to be enforceable. A promise to do something for nothing is usually not a contract at all.

Make each side's consideration clear and recorded: payment, services, mutual promises. There are limited exceptions, including certain written and registered promises made on account of natural love and affection, and they are not something to build a commercial arrangement on.

Inadequate stamping

This is the quiet killer, because nothing about it shows until you need the document.

An agreement that is not adequately stamped under the applicable stamp law can be inadmissible as evidence, so a perfectly valid contract may not help you in court until the deficiency and the penalty are paid.

Pay the correct duty for your state and document type, use e-stamping where it is available, and treat it as part of execution rather than as an afterthought.

Void restraints of trade

Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act makes an agreement that restrains a person from carrying on a lawful profession, trade or business void.

Broad post-employment non-competes and sweeping restrictions are therefore generally unenforceable, with narrow exceptions including the genuine sale of a business's goodwill and reasonable restraints during the term of employment.

Drafting an unenforceable restraint is worse than leaving it out, because it produces confidence in protection that does not exist. Use enforceable tools such as confidentiality instead.

E-signing the wrong documents

Electronic signatures are valid in India for most contracts under the IT Act, and not for everything.

Excluded documents cannot be validly e-signed: negotiable instruments other than a cheque, a power of attorney, a trust deed, a will, and any sale deed or conveyance of immovable property.

E-signing one of these does not produce a defective document so much as no document at all. Know the exclusions before relying on a digital signature.

No dispute resolution or jurisdiction

Where a contract is silent on how and where disputes are resolved, you inherit slow and uncertain litigation in a forum you did not choose.

Include a clear dispute resolution clause specifying arbitration or courts, the seat or venue, and the governing law and jurisdiction. It is a short clause that saves a great deal when a dispute actually arrives.

A worked example

A company signs a consultancy agreement saying the consultant will "provide strategic support" for a fee "payable on completion", with a clause barring the consultant from working in the industry for three years afterwards, signed electronically on plain paper.

Almost every element is a problem. The scope is vague. "Completion" is an undefined payment trigger. The three-year non-compete is likely void under Section 27. The agreement is inadequately stamped. There is no dispute resolution clause.

When payment is disputed, the company struggles to enforce its own contract. A redraft with specific deliverables, milestone payments, confidentiality in place of the non-compete, proper stamping and an arbitration clause would make it solid — and would have taken an afternoon.

Common mistakes

  • Ambiguous scope and undefined payment triggers.
  • Missing IP, termination, liability or dispute clauses.
  • Inadequate stamping, risking inadmissibility.
  • Void non-competes under Section 27.
  • E-signing excluded documents such as wills, property deeds and powers of attorney.
  • No governing law or jurisdiction.

A drafting checklist

  1. Make every key term specific and objectively testable.
  2. Work from a clause checklist so nothing essential is missing.
  3. Ensure clear consideration on both sides.
  4. Pay the correct stamp duty, e-stamping where available.
  5. Avoid void restraints, and use confidentiality instead of a broad non-compete.
  6. Do not e-sign an excluded document. Add governing law, jurisdiction and dispute resolution, and sign and date properly.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a contract unenforceable in India? Missing essential terms, lack of consideration, inadequate stamping, void restraints of trade, and e-signing documents that require a physical signature.

Can an unstamped agreement be used in court? An inadequately stamped document may be inadmissible until the duty and penalty are paid, which stalls the case at the worst moment.

Are non-compete clauses valid in India? Broad post-employment non-competes are generally void under Section 27, with narrow exceptions such as the genuine sale of business goodwill.

Which documents cannot be signed electronically? Negotiable instruments other than cheques, powers of attorney, trust deeds, wills, and sale deeds or conveyances of immovable property.

Do I need a dispute resolution clause? Yes. Without one you face slow and uncertain litigation. A clause naming arbitration or courts, the venue and the governing law saves both time and cost.

Our contract is already signed and has these problems. What now? Most are fixable by an amendment or an addendum, and stamping deficiencies can be cured by paying the duty and penalty. Do it before a dispute rather than during one.
