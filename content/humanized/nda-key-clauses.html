Someone downloads an NDA template, fills in two names, and assumes they are protected. A weak NDA is worse than none, because it buys confidence while leaving open the gaps that actually matter — and the clause people rely on most, the one stopping the other side from competing afterwards, is usually void in India.

An NDA is enforceable in India when the definition of confidential information is precise, the exclusions are stated, the use is limited to a named purpose, and it provides for an injunction — but a broad post-employment non-compete inside it is void under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act.

The bottom line

What makes it hold: a tight definition of confidential information, the standard exclusions, a stated permitted use, a term, a return or destruction obligation, and the right to seek an injunction.

Mutual or one-way: pick the one matching the actual flow of information. Signing a one-way NDA when you will also be sharing your own secrets leaves you exposed.

What it cannot do: function as a non-compete. Confidentiality survives; a sweeping post-employment restraint generally does not.

What an NDA is for

A contract in which one or both parties agree not to disclose or misuse information shared during a relationship — a hire, an investment conversation, a vendor engagement, a partnership discussion.

Its job is narrow and useful: to let you share enough to do business without losing control of the thing that makes your business valuable.

Mutual or one-way

A one-way or unilateral NDA covers information disclosed by a single party, with the other agreeing to protect it. That suits briefing a contractor, or pitching an investor.

A mutual or bilateral NDA binds both sides because both are sharing. That suits two companies exploring a partnership.

Match it to the real flow of information rather than to whichever template arrived first.

The clauses you cannot skip

  • Definition of confidential information — specific enough to mean something: technical data, business plans, customer lists, pricing, code, and ideally covering written, oral and visual disclosures.
  • Exclusions — information already public, already known to the recipient, independently developed, or rightfully received from a third party. Courts expect these, and their absence signals a template nobody read.
  • Permitted use — the information may be used only for the stated purpose and nothing else.
  • Obligations of the recipient — keep it secret, limit access to need-to-know people, do not copy or reverse-engineer.
  • Term — commonly 2 to 5 years, or indefinite for genuine trade secrets.
  • Return or destruction — on termination or request, the recipient returns or destroys the material.
  • Remedies — the right to seek an injunction, because damages rarely undo a leak, plus indemnity for losses.
  • Governing law and jurisdiction — whose courts decide it.
  • Carve-out for legally compelled disclosure — under a court order, with notice to the disclosing party.

The non-compete trap

This is where Indian NDAs go wrong. People bundle in a clause stopping the recipient from competing, or working for a rival, after the relationship ends.

Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act makes an agreement that restrains someone from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business void. So a broad post-employment non-compete is generally unenforceable here. Restraints during employment, and restraints in the genuine sale of a business's goodwill, are treated differently.

Confidentiality survives that section. A sweeping non-compete usually does not, and a template that contains one will not tell you so. The enforceability question is worth understanding properly before you rely on the clause.

What makes it enforceable

A confidentiality obligation holds when it is reasonable, specific and supported by consideration.

Keep the definition tight, because an over-broad NDA invites challenge. Make the purpose explicit. Include the injunction remedy. Ensure the document is properly signed and stamped for your state. Keep the term reasonable.

The narrower and clearer the document, the more likely a court enforces it — which is the opposite of the instinct that produces most templates.

A worked example

A startup is about to share its codebase and customer data with a freelance developer.

Instead of a generic one-pager it uses a one-way NDA that defines confidential information to include the code, the architecture and the customer data; excludes anything public or independently known; limits use strictly to the agreed project; requires deletion of all copies on completion; gives the startup the right to an injunction if the developer leaks or reuses the data; and names the courts of its home city.

It deliberately leaves out a post-employment non-compete, relying on enforceable confidentiality instead. When the project ends the data comes back and the secrets stay secret.

Common mistakes

  • A vague definition of confidential information, which protects nothing.
  • No exclusions, which courts may read in anyway and which signals sloppy drafting.
  • Relying on a non-compete that Section 27 likely makes void.
  • No injunction remedy, leaving only slow and inadequate damages.
  • Forgetting return or destruction, so copies linger long after the deal ends.

Frequently asked questions

Is an NDA legally enforceable in India? Yes. A well-drafted confidentiality obligation is enforceable, provided it is reasonable, specific and properly executed.

What is the difference between a mutual and a one-way NDA? A one-way NDA protects information disclosed by a single party. A mutual NDA binds both, because both are sharing.

Can an NDA stop an employee joining a competitor? Generally no. A broad post-employment non-compete is void under Section 27, and only the confidentiality obligation reliably survives.

How long should an NDA last? Commonly 2 to 5 years, though genuine trade secrets can be protected indefinitely.

What remedy do I have on breach? An injunction to stop further disclosure, and damages or indemnity for losses where the NDA provides for them.

Does an NDA need to be stamped? Yes, per your state's schedule. An unstamped or under-stamped agreement runs into admissibility problems exactly when you need to produce it.
